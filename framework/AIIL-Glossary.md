# AIIL Glossary

Version: 1.0 Draft

## Purpose

This glossary establishes the official vocabulary of the Artificial Intelligence Infrastructure Library (AIIL).

The objective is to create a common language for executives, managers, governance teams, architects, engineers, auditors, and AI practitioners.

---

# A

## AI Agent

A software entity capable of performing tasks, making decisions, or executing workflows using Artificial Intelligence.

---

## AI Asset

Any component that contributes to an AI solution.

Examples:

- Models

- Prompts

- Agents

- Datasets

- Vector Databases

- Workflows

---

## AI Assessment

The process of evaluating an organization's AI maturity using the AIIL framework.

---

# B

## Business Value

The measurable benefit generated by an AI initiative.

Examples:

- Cost reduction

- Productivity improvement

- Risk reduction

- Revenue growth

---

# C

## Continual Improvement

The ongoing effort to improve AI capabilities, governance, performance, and business outcomes.

---

## Compliance

The state of adhering to regulations, policies, standards, and internal requirements.

---

# D

## Data Classification

The process of categorizing information according to sensitivity and protection requirements.

---

## Data Governance

The management of data quality, ownership, security, and compliance.

---

# G

## Governance

The framework of policies, responsibilities, controls, and decision-making structures used to manage AI.

---

## Governance Board

The group responsible for overseeing AI strategy, risk, policy, and compliance.

---

# H

## Hallucination

An AI-generated response that contains fabricated, inaccurate, or unsupported information.

---

# I

## AI Incident

Any event that degrades or disrupts the expected operation of an AI service.

---

## Incident Record

The official documentation of an AI incident.

---

## AI Initiative

A project, program, or effort involving the use of Artificial Intelligence to achieve business objectives.

---

# K

## Knowledge Base

A structured collection of information used to support AI systems, agents, and users.

---

## Known Error

A documented problem with an identified root cause and potential workaround.

---

# M

## Maturity Level

The degree to which an organization has institutionalized AI governance and operational practices.

---

## Model

A machine learning or generative AI system used to perform specific tasks.

---

## Model Lifecycle

The stages through which an AI model progresses:

- Selection

- Development

- Deployment

- Operation

- Improvement

- Retirement

---

# O

## Owner

The individual accountable for an AI service, model, or agent.

---

## Operational Risk

The risk of business disruption caused by failures in AI processes, systems, or governance.

---

# P

## Prompt

Instructions provided to an AI model to influence outputs.

---

## Prompt Library

A managed collection of approved prompts and prompt templates.

---

## Problem

The root cause of one or more AI incidents.

---

## Problem Record

Documentation used to track investigation and resolution of recurring issues.

---

# R

## Risk

The possibility that an AI-related event negatively impacts business objectives.

---

## Risk Register

A repository used to document and track identified risks.

---

## Root Cause Analysis

A structured investigation designed to identify the underlying cause of an incident or problem.

---

# S

## AI Service

A business capability enabled through Artificial Intelligence.

---

## Service Owner

The individual accountable for the delivery and performance of an AI service.

---

## Security Control

A safeguard implemented to reduce security risks associated with AI usage.

---

## Score

A numerical result produced by an AIIL assessment.

---

# V

## Vector Database

A specialized database used to store and retrieve embeddings for AI applications.

---

# W

## Workflow

A sequence of activities executed by humans, AI systems, or agents to achieve a desired outcome.

---

# AIIL Maturity Terminology

## Level 1 - Experimental

Informal AI usage with minimal controls.

---

## Level 2 - Controlled

Basic governance and approved tools.

---

## Level 3 - Managed

Documented processes and defined ownership.

---

## Level 4 - Governed

Formal governance, compliance, and risk management.

---

## Level 5 - Optimized

Continuous improvement and strategic AI enablement.
