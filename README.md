# Artificial Intelligence Infrastructure Library

AIIL (Artificial Intelligence Infrastructure Library) provides the governance foundation, operational framework, and shared vocabulary needed to manage Artificial Intelligence at enterprise scale.

Website: [aiilframework.com](https://aiilframework.com/)

## Mission

Organizations are deploying AI at scale (Generative AI, AI Agents, Copilots, and Large Language Models) without a standardized operational framework.

AIIL was created to address that gap.

AIIL is built for two audiences: executives and managers who need to govern AI as a business capability, and AI practitioners who need a common standard to operate AI services at scale.

## Vision

To become the leading framework for AI Service Management, AI Governance, and AI Operations.

## Core Objectives

- Establish AI governance practices
- Standardize AI operational processes
- Define AI management responsibilities
- Support AI risk management
- Enable continuous improvement
- Align AI initiatives with business objectives

## AIIL Core Pillars

| # | Pillar | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strategy | Align AI initiatives with business objectives and measurable outcomes |
| 2 | Governance | Define ownership, accountability, policies, and decision-making structures |
| 3 | Data | Manage data quality, privacy, classification, and usage policies |
| 4 | Models | Establish standards for selecting, evaluating, and governing AI models |
| 5 | Agents | Manage AI agents, automations, responsibilities, and lifecycle |
| 6 | Operations | Operate AI services using structured, repeatable processes |
| 7 | Security | Protect AI systems against misuse, data leakage, and prompt injection |
| 8 | Continual Improvement | Continuously improve AI performance, adoption, governance, and business value |

## AIIL Core Processes

| Process | Purpose |
|---|---|
| AI Governance Management | Ensure alignment with policies, strategy, and regulatory requirements |
| AI Incident Management | Restore normal AI operations after failures or unexpected behavior |
| AI Problem Management | Identify and eliminate root causes of recurring AI incidents |
| AI Change Management | Control all modifications to AI systems and services |
| AI Agent Management | Manage the complete lifecycle of AI agents |
| AI Risk Management | Identify, evaluate, and treat AI-related risks |
| AI Knowledge Management | Ensure the right AI knowledge is available to the right people |

## Maturity Model

AIIL defines five levels of organizational AI maturity:

| Level | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Experimental | AI used informally, no governance, no controls |
| 2 | Controlled | Basic controls and approved tools exist |
| 3 | Managed | Defined ownership and standardized processes |
| 4 | Governed | Formal governance, risk management, compliance monitoring |
| 5 | Optimized | Continuous improvement and strategic business alignment |
